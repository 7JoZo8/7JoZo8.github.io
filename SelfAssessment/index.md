## Professional Self-Assessment

Throughout my time in the Computer Science program at Southern New Hampshire University, I have developed a stronger understanding of software development, problem-solving, and how different technologies connect and work together to create functional applications. When I first entered the program, I was primarily focused on learning how to write code and make individual programs work correctly. As I progressed through my coursework, I began to understand the importance of looking at the bigger picture when developing software. I learned to consider how different components interact, how software can be designed to remain reliable, efficient, and maintainable, and how technical decisions affect the overall application. Completing this ePortfolio has given me the opportunity to reflect on that growth while improving projects that represent important skills I developed throughout my degree.

An area in which I have grown is collaboration and considering different perspectives when solving technical problems. Although my coursework did not involve extensive group assignments, I participated in class discussions where I could exchange ideas with other students taking the same course. Having these conversations provided me with opportunities to share my own approaches to programming while learning from the experiences and perspectives of my classmates. One of the most valuable lessons I took from the discussions was the importance of thinking about the bigger picture instead of focusing entirely on an individual section of code. I have applied this mindset throughout my coursework by considering how programming decisions affect other components of an application. These experiences helped me recognize how collaboration and exchanging ideas can contribute to more informed decisions and better solutions.

Outside of the projects included in my ePortfolio, my coursework also helped me develop important habits for approaching programming challenges. Learning to use pseudocode gave me ways to plan how a program should function before starting any actual coding process. I also gained experience with debugging and testing through assignment that required me to identify problems, evaluate possible solutions, and verify that my changes worked correctly. My cyber-security related coursework introduced me to additional considerations involving secure computing practices and information technology systems. These experiences helped me become more thoughtful about planning, developing, and maintaining software.

Communication has also become an important part of my development as a computer science student. I learned that understanding a technical concept is only part of the challenge. Being able to explain that concept clearly is just as important. My ePortfolio demonstrates this through my code review video, enhancement narratives, technical documentation, and descriptions of my completed projects. Preparing these materials required me to explain why particular improvements were necessary, how I implemented them, and what the results of my improvements demonstrated. I also learned to present technical information in ways that could be understood by audiences with different levels of programming experience. These communication skills will be valuable in a professional environment where developers need to explain problems, discuss solutions, and document their work for others.

One of the most rewarding aspects of the Computer Science program was learning how programming logic can be used to create something both functional and visually engaging. I particularly enjoyed working on my CS 360 Inventory Management Application and my CS 330 Computational Visualization 3D scene. Both projects gave me opportunities to develop functionality behind the scenes that translated into something a user could see or interact with. CS 330 was by far my favorite course throughout the program. I spent countless hours developing and refining my OpenGL scene because I genuinely enjoyed seeing how programming, object transformations, textures, and rendering techniques come together to create a three-dimensional environment. This project allowed me to combine technical problem-solving with creativity, and it reinforced how much I enjoyed developing underlying logic that makes an application work.

For the Algorithms and Data Structures category on my ePortfolio, I returned to the CS 330 project to improve the efficiency and organization of its internal code. My enhancement introduced a structured collection of scene objects and a reusable approach to rendering them. I also implemented a hash-based lookup using C++’s unordered_map to make it easier to locate specific objects within the scene. This gave me an opportunity to compare sequential searching, which has O(n) time complexity, with hash-based lookup, which provides average O(1) lookup time. I performed benchmarking to evaluate these approaches and better understand their performance differences. More importantly, this project taught me that selecting a computing solution requires considering trade-offs such as efficiency, memory usage, scalability, and maintainability. The enhancement allowed me to improve the program’s internal design while preserving the visual scene.

My experience with software engineering and security is demonstrated through my CS 360 Inventory Management Application. For this enhancement, I focused on improving the application’s architecture, input handling, and authentication. The original application included account management and inventory functionality using Android and SQLite. I improved the security by implementing salted password hashing using PBKDF2 instead of relying on less secure password storage. I also strengthened input validation and separated application responsibilities to make the code more maintainable and organized. Another part of the enhancement was testing; I used automated tests to evaluate the improved functionality of the enhancement. This project helped me understand that software development involves more than just implementing features. It also requires considering how an application protects information, responds to unexpected inputs, and maintains reliable behavior. These are important skills for developing secure software systems.

The Databases category of my ePortfolio provided another opportunity to apply my growing interest in backend development. For this enhancement, I selected my CS 340 Animal Shelter Dashboard, which uses Python, MongoDB, and Dash to retrieve and present animal shelter records. I expanded the original filtering system by creating dynamic MongoDB queries that support combinations of the following search criteria: animal type, breed, sex, age range, and rescue category. I also created indexes on four frequently searched database fields and used MongoDB’s explain() functionality to evaluate query behavior. During testing, an indexed query examined 608 documents compared with 10,000 documents during a forced collection scan, representing a 93.92% reduction in examined documents. However, the indexed query took slightly longer to execute during that particular test. This was a learning experience for me because it demonstrated that reducing the amount of database work does not always produce faster execution times. I also tested conflicting search criteria to confirm that the application could handle searches returning no results without producing an error. These improvements strengthened my understanding of database functionality, performance evaluation, and the relationship between backend systems and user-facing applications.

Across all three enhancements, I have developed a greater appreciation for using appropriate programming languages, tools, and development practices to solve different types of problems. My work involved Kotlin and SQLite for Android development, C++ and OpenGL for computational graphics, and Python and MongoDB for database-driven applications. Each of these projects required me to evaluate an existing solution, identify areas for improvement, implement changes, and test the results. Revisiting these projects that I completed earlier in my coursework allowed me to recognize how much my approach to programming has changed. Rather than focusing exclusively on making the code work, I have become more conscious of software architecture, maintainability, performance trade-offs, and security considerations.

As I prepare to complete my degree, my primary career goal is to pursue backend software development. I enjoy understanding how an application works behind the scenes, particularly how data is stored, retrieved, and communicated between different components. I am also interested in cybersecurity and networking, especially since backend systems often involve protecting sensitive information and maintaining reliable communication between services. My cybersecurity-focused coursework and the security improvements made to my inventory application have helped me recognize the importance of considering potential vulnerabilities during software development. I would like to continue to improve my knowledge of secure systems while building my skills as a backend developer.

Overall, my experience in the Computer Science program at Southern New Hampshire University has helped me grow both technically and professionally. I have gained practical experience with programming, software architecture, algorithms, data structures, databases, testing, technical communication, and secure development practices. I have also learned to approach software development with greater patience, curiosity, and consideration for how individual decisions affect an entire system. My ePortfolio represents my growth through the three enhanced artifacts that demonstrate my ability to revisit existing software and make meaningful improvements. I am proud of the work I have completed during my degree and during this course, and I look forward to continuing to develop my skills as I pursue a career in backend development.
